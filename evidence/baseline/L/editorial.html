The Coast at Low Tide

Field report · Mara Vega

At the foot of the slipway, a scallop shell sits full of rain. A line of bladderwrack marks where the water stood this morning. Beyond it the sand shines like the inside of a spoon.

From up on the road the shore looked empty. Down here a shrimp flicks backwards into the shadow of a rock. Under a ledge, mussels hang in a dark bunch, their shells held shut against the air. The smallest sounds carry now that the water has withdrawn.

At the kitchen window above the harbour, a cook lifts a lid. The mussels in her pot came from the morning market. She spoons their liquor through a fine sieve, tastes it and sets the salt back on its shelf. A curl of lemon peel goes in instead.

We eat with the window open. By the time the bread has soaked up the last of the broth, the shell at the foot of the slipway has slipped below the water.
