# Comparison scoring

Score the same brief across arms. Each evaluator sees lettered screenshots at 1440 and 390 with corresponding grayscale blur images. The generator and arm are concealed during rating. The project facts and intended action are provided. Rate each visible criterion 0, 1, or 2, with a short reason.

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Task focus | Purpose or next action is hard to find | The action is clear after reading | The most useful content and action are apparent at first glance |
| Distinctiveness | Generic composition could apply to most other briefs | One domain-specific region changes the structure | The visual hierarchy is tied to the subject and identifiable under blur |
| Craft | Competing type, color or borders compromise reading | Coherent but conventional | Deliberate scale, proportion, space and treatment support the task |
| At 390 px | Overflow, obscured control or unreadable details | Usable with minor friction | Clear order and visible, legible key action |
| Evidence in use | Vague or fabricated claims replace project facts | The project facts are all there | The available facts do useful design work in the focal region |

Store ties. Record tool counts, actual timing where available, source findings, font load, overflow and action verification separately. A static tell count is a specific coded measure. It does not stand in for task or visual scores. For the three paired pilot cases, require higher independent preference or same preference with a demonstrated improvement in tell counts and equal task completion. A miss or tie on both dimensions calls for targeted fixes and reruns. Generalisation needs held-out briefs. This pilot does not establish superiority over other products.
