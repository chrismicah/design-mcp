# Visual perception and UI judgments

| Evidence | Product decision | Evaluation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Proximity, similarity, common region and connectedness affect perceptual grouping. Figure–ground assignment also depends on attention and prior experience. | Group by task through spacing and aligned edges. Use a rule or background when group membership needs further clarification. Give each view a dominant figure. | At a blur and at 390 px, ask a rater to point to the primary region and two related groups. Compare with the intended hierarchy. [Wagemans et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0029333) |
| Processing-fluency theory proposes that ease of processing can increase aesthetic pleasure. Repetition and contrast are among its inputs. Screenshot studies found first-impression appeal varied with visual complexity and prototypicality as early as 50 ms. | Use recognizable controls and content order. Concentrate unusual visual treatment in the main idea. Whitespace can increase the difference between within-group and between-group spacing, making relationships easier to read. | Test the intended reading order and first action against an equally legible conventional version. Separately record perceived polish and task success. [Reber, Schwarz and Winkielman, 2004](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15327957pspr0804_3) · [Tuch et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2012.06.003) |
| Cognitive-load work on problem solving motivates reducing unnecessary search during use. The paper tests learning conditions, so application to page composition is a hypothesis. | Keep state and next action visible at the point of decision. Add density for expert workflows by exposing the data they compare in place. | Measure the first correct action and missteps for a plausible task. [Sweller, 1988](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1202_4) |
| The von Restorff isolation effect concerns recall of a distinct item among similar items. Transfer to salience and recall in an interface should be tested. | Reserve the strongest color or scale for one primary region or action. Keep alerts and status independently legible without color. | In a short exposure, ask which action or artifact the rater remembers. Check that it matches the actual goal. [Von Restorff, 1933](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02409636) |
| Typicality and novelty jointly explained product preference in three industrial-design studies. | Keep a recognizable product skeleton and choose one novel visual decision rooted in its subject. Reuse report conventions in a dashboard and place the distinctiveness in how one valuable comparison is made. | Compare hierarchy and screenshot silhouette across six unrelated tasks. Test usability separately. [Hekkert, Snelders and van Wieringen, 2003](https://doi.org/10.1348/000712603762842147) |

“Looks expensive” is an audience-specific interpretation. Test whether the intended viewer reads generous margins, aligned detail and carefully reproduced imagery as care or as sparse content. Default toward clarity and coherence, then test the brand impression with examples and first-action tasks. Keep contrast, keyboard behavior, zoom, and reduced-motion preferences as separate hard checks. [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)
