# Why generated interfaces converge

**Design MCP should measure whether different jobs produce different page structures.** A page can be attractive, readable and appropriate to the category yet look interchangeable beside its peers. In a study of 73 promotional websites for different businesses, 25 used a centered headline, navigation and call to action. Fifteen of those heroes were dark and ten were light. Counting palettes alone would give the generator credit for diversity while missing the repeated composition. [Cohort study, Appendix A.5](../evidence/research/market/studies.md#s2)

## What the starting paper establishes

The PDF masthead verifies **arXiv:2603.13036v1 [cs.HC], 13 March 2026**, *Interrogating Design Homogenization in Web Vibe Coding*, by Donghoon Shin, Alice Gao, Rock Yuren Pang, Jaewook Lee, Katharina Reinecke and Emily Tseng. It concerns people prompting tools to build websites. The authors reviewed 13 academic and 50 grey-literature sources. The lead author walked six tools over one week, including v0, Lovable and Claude **Artifacts**. Three authors reviewed the walkthrough records. The result is a workflow and harm analysis. Its illustrative cases support hypotheses about how convergence happens. They supply neither a prevalence estimate nor a ranked comparison of generators. [Versioned PDF, abstract and full-text receipts](../evidence/research/market/studies.md#s1)

Shin et al. locate decisions from tool choice and prompting through code generation, preview, revision and deployment. A user can specify a goal, see an apparently finished page, then spend every subsequent turn adjusting choices the system made silently. The authors propose **productive friction** at three levels. Give an individual a meaningful visual choice, anchor team output in its actual brand system, and preserve the provenance of different design traditions. Their example of a model rationalizing a sparse page as appropriate for a Japanese audience is especially instructive. A plausible explanation can conceal a poor assumption. [Figure 1 and §§3–5](../evidence/research/market/studies.md#s1)

## Where the explanation holds up

| Evidence | What it changes about the rebuild |
|---|---|
| **Measured generated websites.** Boussioux, Zhao and Cho studied 73 sites built with Lovable in one graduate class. One clustering specification assigned 64 sites to six groups. Across 60 specifications the number ranged from three to twelve. The largest named group had 21 hero-plus-card-grid sites. Reported control, satisfaction and quality showed no detectable association with measured visual atypicality. | Put outputs beside other outputs. A happy builder looking at one site cannot evaluate the distribution. The shared assignment, tool, instructor and genre could all contribute. The authors identify the missing matched human/template control and the need for human validation of visual similarity. [Study §§3.5, 4, 6 and 7](../evidence/research/market/studies.md#s2) |
| **Human-built websites already converged.** Goree et al. studied website images from 2003–2019 and interviewed 11 professionals. Average layout distance fell by over 30% after 2007. Common libraries, palettes and mobile constraints appear in the authors’ account. | Compare with a template and human-designed sites **from the same genre**. Shared conventions can be useful for checkout or navigation. Test distinctiveness in the composition around them. [CHI 2021 source](../evidence/research/market/studies.md#s3) |
| **A better individual result can accompany a narrower group.** Doshi and Hauser’s controlled short-story experiment found higher creativity ratings alongside greater similarity across AI-assisted stories. Anderson et al.’s 36-person ideation study found more detailed ideas but less semantic distance between users’ ideas with ChatGPT than with another creativity tool. | Score fit to the brief and cross-brief repetition separately. These studies concern writing and ideas; their effect sizes cannot be transferred to websites. [Story experiment](../evidence/research/market/studies.md#s5) · [Ideation study](../evidence/research/market/studies.md#s6) |

## Four places a default enters

1. **The model fills in what the brief leaves open.** An autoregressive model chooses a token sequence conditional on context. The decoding strategy can affect repetition even for a fixed language model. “Regression to the mean” is an informal description of recognizable conventions recurring. It is not an arithmetic average of screenshots or proof about how often any font appeared in a proprietary training set. A vague request for “a premium dashboard” leaves the focal decision, information density and evidence of credibility unresolved. Supply one task that must be legible at a glance. [Language modeling and decoding](../evidence/research/market/studies.md#s10) · [Seed workflow](../evidence/research/market/studies.md#s1)
2. **Retrieval and scaffolding narrow the choices again.** Design MCP’s packaged search scores matching query terms and breaks ties by stored quality. Its design-system tool defaults to a dark dashboard. A competitor’s public rule for “SaaS (General)” recommends a hero, feature section, CTA, glassmorphism and trust blue. Repeated results can therefore originate in retrieval or explicit rules even with the same model. Compare the chosen reference IDs, skeleton and defaults for multiple briefs before blaming sampling. [Local source](../evidence/research/market/README.md#local-baseline) · [Pinned competitor rule](../evidence/research/market/pro-max-rule-counts.json)
3. **The first preview becomes the reference for the next turn.** The later cohort study examined nine recorded sessions. It documents intent getting lost between speech and prompts and few explicit checks for originality. That process analysis is preliminary. An agent can cheaply test a missing choice by stating a default in terms of visible consequences: “I’ll put the next departure and platform above the route map.” Someone who says “map first” has given actionable direction without having to name a layout style. [Process evidence and limitations](../evidence/research/market/studies.md#s2)
4. **A cure can become the next template.** Of 72 sites available for font inspection in the cohort, 24 specified Playfair Display for headings and 12 specified Cormorant Garamond. Anthropic’s public frontend-design skill itself calls out the cream/serif/clay combination. A carefully chosen display serif can still suit a brief. Rotating font names while keeping the same hero, image crop and card rhythm will miss the observed problem. [Typography, Appendix A.7](../evidence/research/market/studies.md#s2) · [Published skill guidance](../evidence/research/market/products.md#m21)

## How to test the explanation in Design MCP

These are proposed experiments for the rebuild. Fix the client, model version, content, assets and sampling settings across conditions. Use a plain-agent control, a strong public design skill, the current MCP and the candidate. Archive prompts, retrieved IDs, direction, revisions and screenshots. Randomize their order for independent reviewers. The source studies justify cross-output comparison. The thresholds in the [competitive acceptance tests](RESEARCH-COMPETITORS.md#beat-them-list) are project decisions. [Evaluation precedent](../evidence/research/market/studies.md#s9)

| Change one input | Expected observable result | What would challenge the explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Give the same vague brief for a dance performance and a freight exception desk. Supply dates/cast imagery for one and cutoff times/unresolved shipments for the other. | The first screen leads with a performance and its date in one case, a prioritized exception and deadline in the other. Visible type, density and mobile order support those tasks. | Both reduce to a centered proposition followed by interchangeable benefit cards after logos and color are masked. |
| Hold brief and content fixed. Replace popularity-first reference retrieval with references matched by task, image availability and layout structure. | Different reference IDs yield an identifiable change in composition while reviewers still find the primary action. | Reference IDs diversify while the screenshots retain the same geometry, or the task becomes harder. Inspect the direction-to-implementation step. |
| Start with a deliberately vague or contradictory request. Give a sensible default and at most two short choices. Capture the predicted change for a one-word reaction before applying it. | “Denser” on an exception desk reveals more actionable rows at the same viewport. “Photo-led” on a performance listing moves the supplied image and date into the first reading group. | Only colors, adjectives or decoration change. The mobile view loses the primary action or the user has to restate the brief. |
| Compare full pages, first viewports and 390 px mobile views with real examples from the same genre. Hide brands, then review the originals for legibility, copy and task completion. | Reviewers can identify which job each screen serves and name the evidence. A repeated useful control can survive the review. | Color alone carries differentiation, or preserving an unusual silhouette prevents completion of the task. |

The first rebuild slice worth testing is one direction from the user's nouns, one traceable reference principle, and one explicit prediction about what should appear above the fold on a phone. A blind reviewer should be able to verify all three in the resulting screen.
